# The island — what it's for

Written for the sessions of Claude that build the island in a loop (tools/island/loop.sh). Read this
first, every session. Sharpen it when you understand the island better, and say why in the ledger.

## A portrait of Vincent

wingedsheep.com is Vincent's home on the web, and the island is the house: someone who wanders it
should come away knowing what he's like. His dog, his coffee, his kayak, his work, his people, his
books and games, the hours he keeps. It isn't a portfolio with a theme; it's a place he lives in,
and the work is one of the rooms. Every addition answers: *does this make the island more amazing,
and more his?*

What he's told earlier sessions about himself lives in the memory folder the loop prompt points
to. Use it: the best ideas come from real details (the stream from the park he grew up by, the cat
that announces an ambush with one tiny mew), not from what a generic island would have. Never make
up facts about his life, work or people; ask in the ledger instead.

## The feel

- **Cosy and curious.** Somewhere you want to stay a while, where poking at things pays off.
- **Alive.** The island follows the real clock, the season, the visitor's weather and the calendar.
  People and animals have routines and moods. Things happen when you're not looking, and some only
  if you wait. AGENTS.md's "fit the moment" is the heart of it.
- **Secrets, some subtle, some obvious.** A mix: things anyone finds, and things only a patient or
  lucky visitor ever sees. Rare things stay rare.
- **Witty and understated.** Texts say less than they could. Never explain the joke or the
  reference ("this is where wingedsheep got its name" is exactly wrong). One light joke per beat.
  Characters are more than one joke each.
- **Pixel art, always.** Everything goes through the PixelRenderer: chunky texels, outlines, stepped
  and dithered bands, a limited palette. Small things are a few texels big, so check them zoomed in.
- **Made with care.** One thing done properly (its model, its sound, its moment, its words, its
  phone layout) beats three sketched.

## Big things are welcome

The island can grow in every direction: new places to go (rooms, a sea trip, somewhere under the
water, up the mountain, out to the radio ship), games like the river, systems that change how the
whole island lives (the people's days, the wildlife, the weather's effects), music, a better version
of something that's already there. A big thing is a project: write its plan in the ledger, build it
over several sessions, and leave it working at the end of each one.

Make it feel like one island. A new place is reached from the island in a way that makes sense
there (a path, a boat, a door, a telescope), and it shares the island's clock, weather and style.

## Low Water

Claude's own place, past the north cliffs. It has its own vision (tools/lowwater/VISION.md), written
in Claude's voice, and it stays Claude's portrait, not Vincent's. Keep the two apart: Low Water's
quiet and its themes don't spread over the island, and the island's busy life doesn't crowd the flat.

## Words

AGENTS.md's rules hold: the river game says *you*; Eef is named sparingly, mostly *she*; career
text says "worked on" for team work. Also from Vincent: lead with architecture and building, never
leading or teaching; every 3D model on the island was made by AI, so never credit him with them. Keep
it plain English. Read lines aloud: if one sounds like a greeting card or a TED talk, cut it.

## Phones

Many visitors come on a phone. Everything has to work by touch, fit a small screen without covering
the view, and keep a decent frame rate. Check every unit at phone size.
